7. Ordered plate crack propagation¶

What each new name does¶

The following API is experimental in this release candidate. Each operation can be learned independently before running a propagation analysis.

Name Inputs and output
CenterThroughCrack(half_width, half_length) TC01 symmetric center crack. Its stress_intensity(stress) method returns one opening-mode intensity. Both dimensions are half-dimensions.
EdgeThroughCrack(width, length) TC02 single edge crack. Give the full plate width and the distance from the edge to the tip. Its stress_intensity(stress) method returns one opening-mode intensity.
SphereThroughCrack(mean_radius, thickness, half_length) TC06 through-wall arc on a great circle. The half-length follows the mid-wall arc.
AxialCylinderThroughCrack(mean_radius, thickness, half_length) TC07 longitudinal through-wall crack. Use hoop membrane stress to open the crack.
CircumferentialCylinderThroughCrack(mean_radius, thickness, half_length, length, poisson) TC08 through-wall arc in a thin cylinder. The half-length follows the mid-wall arc and length is the full axial cylinder length. Use axial membrane stress.
SemiEllipticalSurfaceCrack(half_width, thickness, depth, half_length) SC01 surface crack. Its stress_intensity(stress) method returns a pair ordered as deepest point, free surface.
CircumferentialCylinderSurfaceCrack(inner_radius, thickness, depth, half_length) SC05 external pipe crack under axial tension. half_length follows the outer-surface arc. Intensities are ordered as deepest point, free surface.
RoundBarSurfaceCrack(diameter, depth) SC07 circular surface crack in a solid bar. Its stress_intensity(stress) method returns one deepest-point intensity for remote tension.
RolledBoltThreadSurfaceCrack(major_diameter, minor_diameter, depth) SC08 thumbnail in a rolled thread. The single intensity is at the deepest point.
HoleBoreSurfaceCrack(width, thickness, hole_diameter, depth, half_length, crack_count) SC11 one or two symmetric cracks at a plate hole. Give the full plate dimensions. Intensities are ordered as radial tip, bore tip.
NasgroCurve Stores calibrated material inputs. get_growth_rate(length, k_min, k_max) evaluates one growth rate in mm/cycle at a crack-front point. This is a material calculation without integration.
LoadBlock(minimum, maximum, cycles) Represents repeated loading between two stress extrema. A sequence of blocks preserves the applied order.
simulate_crack_growth(geometry, curve, loads, ...) Combines the geometry, NASGRO material law, and ordered loads to advance the crack over time.
GrowthResult and GrowthPoint The result contains accepted points and a termination reason. A point records cycles, crack dimensions, per-tip intensities, and the zero-based block index.

Open the plate intensity example or open the NASGRO rate example to see these operations on their own.

Plate solutions and loads¶

The ordered solver uses tensile membrane loads. TC01 uses the finite-width secant solution, TC02 uses Tada's single-edge tension equation for lengths through 0.8 of the plate width, and SC01 uses the Newman–Raju finite-width surface-crack tension solution. TC02 was checked against a published uniform-tension strip benchmark. Tada equation and loading conventions · NASA strip benchmark, Table 3 · Newman–Raju surface crack

Through cracks in thin shells¶

TC06 and TC07 use the FLAGRO 2.0 expressions reproduced in NASA CR-4706, with half_length / sqrt(mean_radius * thickness) limited to 3 and 10, respectively. The inputs to stress_intensity() and LoadBlock are opening membrane stresses in MPa. If starting from pressure p, use p * mean_radius / (2 * thickness) for TC06 and p * mean_radius / thickness for TC07. NASA CR-4706, TC06 · NASA CR-4706, TC07

TC08 uses the Forman–Sanders solution for axial membrane tension. The supported domain requires a total arc angle below 270°, an axial length of at least 2 * mean_radius * sqrt(mean_radius / thickness), and a mean radius at least ten times the wall thickness. The last restriction keeps the implementation in the thin-shell range used for comparison. The bending component and net-section stress for the shell models remain outside this implementation. NASFLA geometry, Fig. 2.2.6 · Rege, Table 8.2

Surface cracks in pipes, bars, threads and plate holes¶

SC05 uses the external-crack, axial-tension factors from Raju and Newman's Table 1, with linear interpolation along each table axis. Supply gross-section axial stress in MPa. With pipe inner radius R, thickness t, radial depth a and outer arc half-length c, the table spans 1 ≤ R/t ≤ 10, 0.2 ≤ a/t ≤ 0.8, and 0.6 ≤ a/c ≤ 1. NASA TM-87594, Table 1

SC07 uses the Forman–Shivakumar round-bar tension factor for 0 < depth < diameter / 2. The solver advances the deepest point and the plot constructs the circular front from depth. This case supports remote tension. Bending and the Appendix B net-section calculation remain outside this implementation. Handbook, Table 11.3.13 · NASFLA Fig. 2.2.52

SC08 covers a thumbnail at first contact in a rolled aerospace bolt thread. Use S₀ = 4F/(πd²) in MPa, where F is the applied axial force in N and d is the minor diameter in mm. Supply the actual tensile extrema, including any known preload. The deepest tip uses K = S₀ F₀ √(πa), with the a/c = 1 column of Table 2 interpolated linearly over 0 < a/d ≤ 0.5. Fig. 2 identifies d as the reference diameter for this branch. The thread cross section is a schematic that preserves the radial depth and surface arc length. Mettu et al., NLR-TP-99313, §5, Table 2 and Fig. 2

SC11 uses Newman and Raju's finite-width tension equations for a centered hole and either one crack or two identical cracks, both at the plate mid-plane. Supply gross-section remote tensile stress in MPa. The public depth is the radial distance into the ligament and half_length is the half-length along the bore. The source calls these c and a, respectively. The fit requires 0.2 ≤ half_length/depth ≤ 2, half_length < thickness/2, 0.5 ≤ hole_diameter/thickness ≤ 2 and (hole_diameter + 2·depth)/width < 0.5. NASA TM-85793, Eqs. 53–64 and Table 1

These additions cover axial or remote tension. Internal SC05 cracks, machined SC08 threads, SC11 pin bearing, and bending need their own load and geometry treatments. Growth stops when the crack leaves the supported domain. The last accepted profile is available without a through-crack transition.

Net-section stress¶

For TC01, TC02 and SC01, geometry.net_section_stress(stress) returns the Appendix B net-section stress in MPa under the supplied nonnegative membrane stress. TC02 and SC01 include bending caused by the shift in the net-section centroid. This method reports a diagnostic. The propagation solver evaluates stress intensity from the applied gross-section stress and the geometry solution. The Appendix B yield and flow checks require separate material inputs.

Plot the geometry, material law and result¶

Call geometry.plot() or geometry.echarts() to draw the initial plate crack. Pass result=result to overlay the last accepted profile. Surface cracks are drawn in the crack plane of the plate, pipe, bar, or thread. If a run stops before accepting a state, the plot shows the starting geometry.

fig, ax = geometry.plot(result=result)
geometry.echarts(result=result)

Call curve.plot() or curve.echarts() to show the long-crack reference curve at stress ratio R = 0 (k_min = 0). Both sweep maximum stress intensity up to toughness and mark that limit with a dashed line. Supply length to apply the finite-crack correction to the growth threshold and k_min to change the minimum stress intensity.

fig, ax = curve.plot()
curve.echarts()

fig, ax = curve.plot(length=4.0, k_min=20.0, scale="loglog")

fig, ax = result.plot()
result.echarts()

A scalar k_max sets a shorter sweep endpoint. Supply an array to evaluate exact sample points, or a one-element array to show one point. With explicit samples, k_min can be one value or an array paired with k_max. Use scale="semilogx" to show zero growth below threshold, or scale="semilogy" for the default logarithmic growth-rate axis.

To compare results on shared axes, use other_result.plot(ax=ax, label="Surface crack"). The plot uses the accepted points in result.history.

Material inputs¶

coefficient and exponent set the scale and power of the growth law. The coefficient depends on the units and the exponent. threshold_zero is the long-crack threshold at stress ratio zero. threshold_fanning controls how the threshold changes with stress ratio. p controls the threshold term, which is disabled at p = 0. toughness is the critical maximum stress intensity. q controls the near-toughness term. The solver's toughness stop still applies at q = 0. constraint and peak_to_flow parameterize the crack-opening law. Both stay fixed for the current run.

The pointwise rate method requires a positive crack length and 0 ≤ k_min < k_max < toughness. The propagation solver handles a zero-range block and checks toughness before evaluating the rate.

Solver controls and result¶

The current solver requires four controls. max_cycle_step caps a single integration step in cycles. length_tolerance controls the absolute difference in mm between one full step and two half steps. cycle_tolerance controls the terminal bracket width in cycles. max_trials is an implementation limit that counts accepted and rejected trials. Exhausting it raises an error. Its design is under review.

termination identifies spectrum exhaustion, a toughness stop, or a geometry limit. final_cycles reports the last accepted cycle count, or zero if no state was accepted. At a stop inside a step, it reports the admissible side of the bracket. Treat the output accordingly when comparing against a reference life.

Use simulate_crack_growth for an ordered sequence of tensile membrane load blocks. Each block carries a minimum stress, a maximum stress, and a fractional cycle count. Preserve the order in which the blocks occur. A binned cycle count does not establish that order.

The plate geometries use crack dimensions and plate dimensions in mm. Use MPa for applied stresses and MPa √mm for stress intensity. A center crack takes crack and plate half-widths. A surface crack takes depth and half-length and returns intensities at the deepest point and the free surface in that order.

The implemented center-crack range is \(0 < a/b \le 0.8\), and the surface fit uses \(0 < a/c \le 1.2\), \(0 < a/t \le 0.8\), and \(0 < c/b < 0.5\). For surface cracks deeper than their half-length, SC01 uses the companion Newman–Raju fit in NASA TM-83200. The solution uses linear-elastic stress intensity in uniform membrane tension. The solver stops at a toughness or geometry limit. Its final point is the last accepted state on the admissible side of the terminal cycle bracket.

NasgroCurve uses the NASGRO 3.0 growth and threshold equations with a fixed, user-supplied toughness and the Newman opening law at nonnegative stress ratios. For SC01 growth at the free surface, the solver scales \(\Delta K\) by \(\beta_R=0.9+0.2R^2-0.1R^4\). Intensity plots, history and the toughness check use the applied intensities. SwRI release notes, pp. 5–6

Supply calibrated material inputs in the stated unit system. Bending, retardation, geometry transitions, and the NASGRO material database are outside this implementation.

The TC01 regression reproduces the published 7075-T6 constant-amplitude life within 0.3% using the rounded inputs in Figures 5 and 8. The SC01 regression checks the crack shape at several depths against the published 2219-T87 tension prediction. It also reproduces the NASA/FLAGRO specimen 5576 pre-breakthrough depth history within 0.01 in at four cycle counts through 40,000 cycles. That comparison uses the reported Walker law at fixed stress ratio. Exact version-specific NASFLA agreement requires a matching input deck and output history.
