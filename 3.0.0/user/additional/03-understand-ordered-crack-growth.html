3. How ordered crack growth works¶

Sequential loading determines the crack dimensions at the start of the next block. The geometry converts each applied load to stress intensity, and the material law uses that intensity to advance the crack.

NASGRO growth law¶

NasgroCurve evaluates growth at a crack-front point from the crack length and the two mode-I stress intensity extrema. The user supplies the material coefficient and exponent, crack-opening parameters, threshold parameters, and toughness. The threshold in the rate law can depend on load ratio and finite crack size. See the material inputs.

NASGRO equation¶

For a tensile cycle with \(0 \le K_\min < K_\max < K_c\), define the load ratio \(R=K_\min/K_\max\) and the opening fraction \(f=K_\mathrm{op}/K_\max\). The effective intensity range is

\[ \Delta K_\mathrm{eff} = \frac{1-f}{1-R}\Delta K = (1-f)K_\max. \]

The NASGRO growth law gives the crack advance per cycle.

\[ \frac{da}{dN} = C\left(\Delta K_\mathrm{eff}\right)^n \frac{\left(1-\Delta K_\mathrm{th}/\Delta K\right)^p} {\left(1-K_\max/K_c\right)^q}. \]

\(C\) and \(n\) parameterize the power-law part of the fit. \(p\) controls the approach to threshold and \(q\) the acceleration near the fixed toughness \(K_c\). The threshold factor is one when \(p=0\). When \(p>0\) and \(\Delta K\le\Delta K_\mathrm{th}\), the growth rate is zero. The near-toughness factor is one when \(q=0\).

Threshold, crack opening, and toughness¶

For the supported nonnegative stress ratios, the Newman opening function is

\[ f = \max\!\left(R, A_0 + A_1 R + A_2 R^2 + A_3 R^3\right). \]

The coefficients depend on the constraint factor and the fitted ratio of peak applied stress to flow stress. With \(A_0=f(0)\) and \(C_\mathrm{th}\) the threshold fanning constant, the threshold used by NasgroCurve is

\[ \Delta K_\mathrm{th}(a,R) = \Delta K_0 \sqrt{\frac{a}{a+a_0}} \left[\frac{(1-A_0)(1-R)}{1-f}\right]^{1+C_\mathrm{th}R}. \]

\(\Delta K_0\) is the long-crack threshold at \(R=0\) and \(a_0=0.0381\,\mathrm{mm}\) is the intrinsic length in the NASGRO 3.0 expression. Taking the long-crack limit makes the square-root factor one. See Mettu et al., §2.1.1.

From geometry and load to stress intensity¶

Under proportional tension, evaluate the chosen geometry at the current crack dimensions, then multiply its factor by the applied stress at each end of the load cycle. For a central through crack of half-length \(a\) in a plate of half-width \(b\), the finite-width correction is

\[ Y(a) = \sqrt{\sec\!\left(\frac{\pi a}{2b}\right)},\qquad K_\min=Y(a)\sigma_\min\sqrt{\pi a},\qquad K_\max=Y(a)\sigma_\max\sqrt{\pi a}. \]

A surface crack can use two factors and return two intensity pairs. Keep the front-point order defined by its geometry. Use the reference stress specified by the case. A geometry factor \(Y\) is dimensionless. A factor carrying \(\sqrt{\mathrm{mm}}\) can instead multiply stress directly to give MPa \(\sqrt{\mathrm{mm}}\).

Advancing two front dimensions¶

For a semi-elliptical plate crack, the deepest point gives the rate of depth \(a\) and the surface point gives the rate of half-length \(c\). An integration trial advances the vector of sizes \(\mathbf{x}=(a,c)\) over a cycle increment \(h\). If \(\mathbf{g}\) denotes the vector of rates at the current load, the trial compares

\[ \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{full}=\mathbf{x}+h\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}), \qquad \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{mid}=\mathbf{x}+\frac{h}{2}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}), \qquad \mathbf{x}_\mathrm{half}=\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{mid} +\frac{h}{2}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{mid}). \]

The solver accepts the two-half-step estimate when the difference in each dimension fits the length tolerance. It reduces the cycle step when the trial exceeds the tolerance or meets a terminal boundary.

Crack geometry and front¶

A through crack spans the thickness. A surface crack opens onto a free surface and has a front inside the material. Several solutions advance two front points independently. The geometry defines depth, half-length, and the order of the reported intensities. See the geometry families and validity limits.

For a semi-elliptical plate crack, a point with angle \(\phi\) along the front has position \(x=c\cos\phi\) across the surface and depth \(z=a\sin\phi\). It reaches the deepest point at \(\phi=\pi/2\).

Net-section stress describes stress in the remaining ligament. For a central through crack with half-length \(a\) in a plate of half-width \(b\), the same tension force acts through the remaining width \(2(b-a)\):

\[ \sigma_\mathrm{net}=\frac{\sigma_\mathrm{gross}}{1-a/b}. \]

The supported plate cases report this diagnostic separately. Use the gross-section stress as the input to their mode-I intensity solutions.

Ordered load block and termination¶

A LoadBlock contains the minimum and maximum tensile stress in MPa and a possibly fractional cycle count. simulate_crack_growth applies the blocks in the supplied order. A binned rainflow histogram cannot recover that sequence.

A GrowthResult records accepted cycle counts, crack dimensions, and per-tip intensity extrema. Spectrum exhaustion means the solver completed the supplied history. A toughness or geometry stop identifies the reached boundary. The final accepted profile is on the admissible side of the terminal cycle bracket.
