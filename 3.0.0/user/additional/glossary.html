Glossary¶

Fatigue¶

Progressive deterioration of a material under cyclic loading. See fatigue loading in wind turbines.

Stress ranges¶

The stress range is identified as Δσ and it is the difference between the maximum and the minimum stress in the fatigue cycle

\[\Delta\sigma = \sigma_{max} - \sigma_{min}\]

Stress Amplitudes¶

Half of the stress range

\[\sigma_{alt} = \frac{\sigma_{max} - \sigma_{min}}{2}\]

Mean stress¶

The mean stress is the average of the maximum and minimum stresses in the fatigue cycle.

\[\sigma_{mean} = \frac{(\sigma_{max} + \sigma_{min})}{2}\]

Load ratio¶

The load ratio is the minimum stress divided by the maximum stress. At a fixed crack size under proportional mode-I loading, the corresponding ratio is \(K_\min/K_\max\).

\[R = \frac{\sigma_{\min}}{\sigma_{\max}}\]

SIF range¶

The mode-I stress intensity factor \(K\) describes the near-tip opening stress field. It has units of stress times the square root of length, such as MPa √mm. Its range is \(\Delta K = K_\max - K_\min\).

\[\Delta K = Y(a)\,\Delta\sigma\sqrt{\pi a}\]

Here \(Y(a)\) is the dimensionless geometry factor, \(a\) is the crack dimension defined by the selected solution, and \(\Delta\sigma\) is the applied stress range. A surface-crack solution can give a different \(K\) at each front point.

Crack growth rate¶

The crack growth rate \(da/dN\) is the advance of a crack dimension per cycle, for example in mm/cycle. Py-Fatigue v3 provides Paris, Walker and NASGRO growth laws. Use material coefficients calibrated in units consistent with the analysis.

Paris' law¶

\[\frac{da}{dN} = C(\Delta K)^m\]

The exponent \(m\) is the slope in log–log coordinates. \(C\) is the coefficient in the chosen unit system. A Walker model adjusts the growth rate for the load ratio. See the crack-growth curve guide.

NASGRO growth law¶

A crack-growth law that uses the crack size and the minimum and maximum mode-I stress intensities. See how the NASGRO equation works.

Crack geometry and front¶

The geometry defines the flaw shape. The front is the boundary of the crack within the material. See crack geometry and intensity.

Ordered load block and termination¶

A load block is a repeated pair of stress extrema and its cycle count. A termination records why the ordered solver stopped. See ordered loads and stop reasons.

Tabulated geometry factor¶

TabulatedGeometryFactor interpolates a dimensionless \(Y\) against one growing crack dimension in get_crack_growth. The last tabulated depth is a geometry limit. See the MERC-3 comparison.

Cycle-counting¶

Extracting fatigue cycles and their stress ranges from a stress history.

Rainflow¶

A family of cycle-counting methods that use ordered stress reversals. See the rainflow explanation and worked trace.

Damage Equivalent Stress Range¶

The damage equivalent stress range (DES) represents linear fatigue damage as a constant stress range. Applying \(N_{eq}\) cycles at the DES produces the same damage as the original signal.

We use the definition by ECN.

\[DES = \left(\frac{\sum_in_i\cdot{}\Delta{}\sigma{}_i^m}{N_{eq}}\right)^{1/m}\]

SN Curve¶

An S–N or Wöhler curve gives the fatigue life in cycles at a given stress range. On one branch of an SNCurve, its base-10 intercept \(b\) and slope \(m\) give

\[ \log_{10} N = b - m\log_{10}\Delta\sigma, \qquad N = 10^b (\Delta\sigma)^{-m}. \]

For a multi-branch curve, use the slope and intercept of the branch containing the stress range. See the S–N curve guide.

Damage Equivalent Moment¶

The damage equivalent moment applies the same principle as the damage equivalent stress range to bending moment.

Palmgren-Miner Rule¶

Rule for linear fatigue, uses the idea of SN curve to determine the fatigue damage of a material or detail under the loading.

\[D = \sum_i(\frac{n_i}{N_i(\Delta{}\sigma_i)})\]

with \(n_i\) the number of cycles of a given stress range \(\sigma_i\). \(N_i\) is the number of permisable cycles according to the SN Curve.

Residuals¶

The ordered turning points left when rainflow counting has closed all cycles it can in the record. Adjacent residual points define half-cycles.

Half-cycles¶

An open stress excursion counted with a weight of 0.5. See residuals.

SHM¶

Structural health monitoring.

Timestamp¶

A timestamp is a time information that is associated with a particular segmented data series. Usually it is represented as a datetime.datetime instance.

LFFD¶

Low-frequency fatigue dynamics. The term refers to larger, slower load excursions that can span multiple recording windows. See how LFFD recovery combines residuals.
