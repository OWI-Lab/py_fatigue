Ordered crack growth¶

Use simulate_crack_growth with a NasgroCurve, a crack geometry, and an ordered sequence of tensile LoadBlock instances. For a walkthrough, see Simulate ordered crack growth. For the equations, see How ordered crack growth works.

Geometry cases and public objects¶

Name Inputs and output
CenterThroughCrack(half_width, half_length) TC01 symmetric center crack. Its stress_intensity(stress) method returns one opening-mode intensity. Both dimensions are half-dimensions.
EdgeThroughCrack(width, length) TC02 single edge crack. Give the full plate width and the distance from the edge to the tip. Its stress_intensity(stress) method returns one opening-mode intensity.
SphereThroughCrack(mean_radius, thickness, half_length) TC06 through-wall arc on a great circle. The half-length follows the mid-wall arc.
AxialCylinderThroughCrack(mean_radius, thickness, half_length) TC07 longitudinal through-wall crack. Use hoop membrane stress to open the crack.
CircumferentialCylinderThroughCrack(mean_radius, thickness, half_length, length, poisson) TC08 through-wall arc in a thin cylinder. The half-length follows the mid-wall arc and length is the full axial cylinder length. Use axial membrane stress.
SemiEllipticalSurfaceCrack(half_width, thickness, depth, half_length) SC01 surface crack. Its stress_intensity(stress) method returns a pair ordered as deepest point, free surface.
CircumferentialCylinderSurfaceCrack(inner_radius, thickness, depth, half_length) SC05 external pipe crack under axial tension. half_length follows the outer-surface arc. Intensities are ordered as deepest point, free surface.
RoundBarSurfaceCrack(diameter, depth) SC07 circular surface crack in a solid bar. Its stress_intensity(stress) method returns one deepest-point intensity for remote tension.
RolledBoltThreadSurfaceCrack(major_diameter, minor_diameter, depth) SC08 thumbnail in a rolled thread. The single intensity is at the deepest point.
HoleBoreSurfaceCrack(width, thickness, hole_diameter, depth, half_length, crack_count) SC11 one or two symmetric cracks at a plate hole. Give the full plate dimensions. Intensities are ordered as radial tip, bore tip.
NasgroCurve Stores calibrated material inputs. get_growth_rate(length, k_min, k_max) evaluates one growth rate in mm/cycle at a crack-front point. This is a material calculation without integration.
LoadBlock(minimum, maximum, cycles) Represents repeated loading between two stress extrema. A sequence of blocks preserves the applied order.
simulate_crack_growth(geometry, curve, loads, ...) Combines the geometry, NASGRO material law, and ordered loads to advance the crack over time.
GrowthResult and GrowthPoint The result contains accepted points and a termination reason. A point records cycles, crack dimensions, per-tip intensities, and the zero-based block index.

Open the plate intensity example or open the NASGRO rate example to see these operations on their own.

Plate solutions and loads¶

The ordered solver uses tensile membrane loads. TC01 uses the finite-width secant solution, TC02 uses Tada's single-edge tension equation for lengths through 0.8 of the plate width, and SC01 uses the Newman–Raju finite-width surface-crack tension solution. TC02 was checked against a published uniform-tension strip benchmark. Tada equation and loading conventions · NASA strip benchmark, Table 3 · Newman–Raju surface crack

Through cracks in thin shells¶

TC06 and TC07 use the FLAGRO 2.0 expressions reproduced in NASA CR-4706, with half_length / sqrt(mean_radius * thickness) limited to 3 and 10, respectively. The inputs to stress_intensity() and LoadBlock are opening membrane stresses in MPa. If starting from pressure p, use p * mean_radius / (2 * thickness) for TC06 and p * mean_radius / thickness for TC07. NASA CR-4706, TC06 · NASA CR-4706, TC07

TC08 uses the Forman–Sanders solution for axial membrane tension. The supported domain requires a total arc angle below 270°, an axial length of at least 2 * mean_radius * sqrt(mean_radius / thickness), and a mean radius at least ten times the wall thickness. The last restriction keeps the implementation in the thin-shell range used for comparison. The bending component and net-section stress for the shell models remain outside this implementation. NASFLA geometry, Fig. 2.2.6 · Rege, Table 8.2

Surface cracks in pipes, bars, threads and plate holes¶

SC05 uses the external-crack, axial-tension factors from Raju and Newman's Table 1, with linear interpolation along each table axis. Supply gross-section axial stress in MPa. With pipe inner radius R, thickness t, radial depth a and outer arc half-length c, the table spans 1 ≤ R/t ≤ 10, 0.2 ≤ a/t ≤ 0.8, and 0.6 ≤ a/c ≤ 1. NASA TM-87594, Table 1

SC07 uses the Forman–Shivakumar round-bar tension factor for 0 < depth < diameter / 2. The solver advances the deepest point and the plot constructs the circular front from depth. This case supports remote tension. Bending and the Appendix B net-section calculation remain outside this implementation. Handbook, Table 11.3.13 · NASFLA Fig. 2.2.52

SC08 covers a thumbnail at first contact in a rolled aerospace bolt thread. Use S₀ = 4F/(πd²) in MPa, where F is the applied axial force in N and d is the minor diameter in mm. Supply the actual tensile extrema, including any known preload. The deepest tip uses K = S₀ F₀ √(πa), with the a/c = 1 column of Table 2 interpolated linearly over 0 < a/d ≤ 0.5. Fig. 2 identifies d as the reference diameter for this branch. The thread cross section is a schematic that preserves the radial depth and surface arc length. Mettu et al., NLR-TP-99313, §5, Table 2 and Fig. 2

SC11 uses Newman and Raju's finite-width tension equations for a centered hole and either one crack or two identical cracks, both at the plate mid-plane. Supply gross-section remote tensile stress in MPa. The public depth is the radial distance into the ligament and half_length is the half-length along the bore. The source calls these c and a, respectively. The fit requires 0.2 ≤ half_length/depth ≤ 2, half_length < thickness/2, 0.5 ≤ hole_diameter/thickness ≤ 2 and (hole_diameter + 2·depth)/width < 0.5. NASA TM-85793, Eqs. 53–64 and Table 1

These additions cover axial or remote tension. Internal SC05 cracks, machined SC08 threads, SC11 pin bearing, and bending need their own load and geometry treatments. Growth stops when the crack leaves the supported domain. The last accepted profile is available without a through-crack transition.

Net-section stress¶

For TC01, TC02 and SC01, geometry.net_section_stress(stress) returns the Appendix B net-section stress in MPa under the supplied nonnegative membrane stress. TC02 and SC01 include bending caused by the shift in the net-section centroid. This method reports a diagnostic. The propagation solver evaluates stress intensity from the applied gross-section stress and the geometry solution. The Appendix B yield and flow checks require separate material inputs.

Material inputs¶

coefficient and exponent set the scale and power of the growth law. The coefficient depends on the units and the exponent. threshold_zero is the long-crack threshold at stress ratio zero. threshold_fanning controls how the threshold changes with stress ratio. p controls the threshold term, which is disabled at p = 0. toughness is the critical maximum stress intensity. q controls the near-toughness term. The solver's toughness stop still applies at q = 0. constraint and peak_to_flow parameterize the crack-opening law. Both stay fixed for the current run.

The pointwise rate method requires a positive crack length and 0 ≤ k_min < k_max < toughness. The propagation solver handles a zero-range block and checks toughness before evaluating the rate.

Solver controls and result¶

The current solver requires four controls. max_cycle_step caps a single integration step in cycles. length_tolerance controls the absolute difference in mm between one full step and two half steps. cycle_tolerance controls the terminal bracket width in cycles. max_trials is an implementation limit that counts accepted and rejected trials. Exhausting it raises an error. Its design is under review.

termination identifies spectrum exhaustion, a toughness stop, or a geometry limit. final_cycles reports the last accepted cycle count, or zero if no state was accepted. At a stop inside a step, it reports the admissible side of the bracket. Treat the output accordingly when comparing against a reference life.

Use simulate_crack_growth for an ordered sequence of tensile membrane load blocks. Each block carries a minimum stress, a maximum stress, and a fractional cycle count. Preserve the order in which the blocks occur. A binned cycle count does not establish that order.

The plate geometries use crack dimensions and plate dimensions in mm. Use MPa for applied stresses and MPa √mm for stress intensity. A center crack takes crack and plate half-widths. A surface crack takes depth and half-length and returns intensities at the deepest point and the free surface in that order.

The implemented center-crack range is \(0 < a/b \le 0.8\), and the surface fit uses \(0 < a/c \le 1.2\), \(0 < a/t \le 0.8\), and \(0 < c/b < 0.5\). For surface cracks deeper than their half-length, SC01 uses the companion Newman–Raju fit in NASA TM-83200. The solution uses linear-elastic stress intensity in uniform membrane tension. The solver stops at a toughness or geometry limit. Its final point is the last accepted state on the admissible side of the terminal cycle bracket.

NasgroCurve uses the NASGRO 3.0 growth and threshold equations with a fixed, user-supplied toughness and the Newman opening law at nonnegative stress ratios. For SC01 growth at the free surface, the solver scales \(\Delta K\) by \(\beta_R=0.9+0.2R^2-0.1R^4\). Intensity plots, history and the toughness check use the applied intensities. SwRI release notes, pp. 5–6

Supply calibrated material inputs in the stated unit system. Bending, retardation, geometry transitions, and the NASGRO material database are outside this implementation.

The TC01 regression reproduces the published 7075-T6 constant-amplitude life within 0.3% using the rounded inputs in Figures 5 and 8. The SC01 regression checks the crack shape at several depths against the published 2219-T87 tension prediction. It also reproduces the NASA/FLAGRO specimen 5576 pre-breakthrough depth history within 0.01 in at four cycle counts through 40,000 cycles. That comparison uses the reported Walker law at fixed stress ratio. Exact version-specific NASFLA agreement requires a matching input deck and output history.

CalcCrackGrowth ¶

Bases: PlotMixin

Crack growth object. This object is used to calculate the crack growth rate and the crack size. The class has been implemented using a Rust kernel to speed up the calculations.

The crack size is numerically integrated over the crack growth curve. In this example we will consider the Paris' law, but the function can be used with any other crack growth curve.

\[ \begin{cases} \frac{da}{dN} & = C \left( \Delta K \right)^{m} \\ \Delta K & = Y(a) \cdot \Delta \sigma \sqrt{\pi a} \\ \end{cases} \]

From the equations above, the crack growths rate can be expressed as a function of the stress range. Considering the Paris' law, as in this example, gives:

\[ \frac{da}{dN} = C \left( Y(a) \cdot \Delta \sigma \sqrt{\pi a} \right)^{m} \]

se this crack growth rate together with the cycle step size to determine the incremental growth of the crack for this iteration:

\[ \Delta a = \frac{da}{dN} \cdot \Delta N \]

The new crack size is then calculated by adding the incremental growth to the previous crack size:

\[ a_{j+1} = a_{j} + \Delta a \]

where \(a_{j}\) is the crack size at the \(j\)-th cycle.

The crack is grown iteratively either until the failure condition is met or until the stress history is over.

If failure occurs, the total number of cycles that it took to grow the crack to the critical size becomes the predicted life of the part.

    function can be used with any other crack growth curve.

    $$
    \\begin{cases}
        \\frac{da}{dN} & = C \\left( \\Delta K \\right)^{m} \\\\
        \\Delta K & = Y(a) \\cdot \\Delta \\sigma \\sqrt{\\pi a} \\\\
    \\end{cases}
    $$

    From the equations above, the crack growths rate can be expressed as a
    function of the stress range. Considering the Paris' law, as in this
    example, gives:

    $$
    \\frac{da}{dN} = C \\left( Y(a) \\cdot \\Delta \\sigma \\sqrt{\\pi a}
        \\right)^{m}
    $$

    se this crack growth rate together with the cycle step size to
    determine the incremental growth of the crack for this iteration:

    $$
    \\Delta a = \\frac{da}{dN} \\cdot \\Delta N
    $$

    The new crack size is then calculated by adding the incremental growth
    to the previous crack size:

    $$
    a_{j+1} = a_{j} + \\Delta a
    $$

    where $a_{j}$ is the crack size at the $j$-th cycle.

    The crack is grown iteratively either until the failure condition
    is met or until the stress history is over.

    If failure occurs, the total number of cycles that it took to grow the
    crack to the critical size becomes the predicted life of the part.


    Parameters
    ----------
    stress_range : np.ndarray
        Stress range array.
    count_cycle : np.ndarray
        Number of cycles array.
    slope : np.ndarray
        Slope array.
    intercept : np.ndarray
        Intercept array.
    threshold : float
        Threshold value.
    critical : float
        Critical value.
    crack_geometry : dict
        Crack geometry dictionary. It must contain the following keys:
        - "initial_depth": float
        - "_id": str



    Returns
    -------
    CalcCrackGrowth
        Crack growth object.
    """

    def __init__(
        self,
        stress_range: np.ndarray,
        count_cycle: np.ndarray,
        slope: np.ndarray,
        intercept: np.ndarray,
        threshold: float,
        critical: float,
        crack_type: str,
        crack_geometry: dict,
    ):

        assert intercept.size > 0 and intercept.size == slope.size
        assert np.min(stress_range) >= 0.0
        assert stress_range.size > 0 and stress_range.size == count_cycle.size
        self.stress_range = stress_range
        self.count_cycle = count_cycle
        self.slope = slope
        self.intercept = intercept
        self.threshold = threshold
        self.critical = critical
        self.crack_type = crack_type
        self.crack_geometry = crack_geometry
        cs, sif, factor, failure = self.calc_crack_depth()
        self.crack_depth = cs
        self.sif = sif
        self.geometry_factor = factor
        self.final_cycles = sum(self.count_cycle[: len(self.crack_depth)])
        self.failure = failure

    def __str__(self) -> str:
        """String representation of the object."""
        if getattr(self, "termination", None) == "geometry_limit":
            return "Crack growth object: geometry table limit reached."
        if self.failure:
            return "Crack growth object: to failure."
        return "Crack growth object: to maximum number of cycles - no failure."

    def plot(
        self, fig: Figure | None = None, ax: Axes | None = None
    ) -> tuple[Figure, Axes]:
        """Plot the crack depth recorded at the start of each legacy step.

        Depths use the same length unit as the supplied geometry.
        """
        fig, ax = make_axes(fig, ax)
        count = len(self.crack_depth)
        cycles = np.r_[0.0, np.cumsum(self.count_cycle[:count])][:count]
        ax.plot(cycles, self.crack_depth, marker="o" if count == 1 else None)
        ax.set(xlabel="Cycles at start of step", ylabel="Crack depth")
        ax.ticklabel_format(axis="y", useOffset=False)
        if not count:
            ax.set_title("No recorded crack depths")
        return fig, ax

    @property
    def size(self) -> int:
        """Size of the problem."""
        return self.stress_range.size

    @property
    def get_knees_sif(self) -> np.ndarray:
        r"""Get the SIF values at the knees of the Paris curve.

        For adjacent Paris branches with coefficients $C_i$ and exponents
        $m_i$, the stress intensity factor range at their intersection is

        $$
        \Delta K_i =
        \left(\frac{C_i}{C_{i+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{m_{i+1} - m_i}}.
        $$
        """
        cg_crv_size = self.intercept.size
        knees_sif = np.empty(
            cg_crv_size - 1, dtype=np.float64
        )  # pylint: disable=E1133, C0301
        if cg_crv_size > 1:  # pylint: disable=E1133, C0301
            for i in range(cg_crv_size - 1):  # pylint: disable=E1133, C0301
                knees_sif[i] = (self.intercept[i] / self.intercept[i + 1]) ** (
                    1 / (self.slope[i + 1] - self.slope[i])
                )
        knees_sif = np.hstack(
            (
                np.array([0.9999999999 * self.threshold]),
                knees_sif,
                np.array([self.critical / 0.9999999999]),
            )
        )
        e_msg = (
            "Knee(s) not in between threshold and critical SIF."
            + "\nCheck the definitions of slope, intercept, "
            + "threshold, critical."
        )
        assert np.all(np.diff(knees_sif) > 0), e_msg
        return knees_sif

    def calc_growth_rate(
        self, stress_, crack_depth_
    ) -> Tuple[float, float, float]:
        """Calculate the crack growth rate given the stress and the
        crack size using the Paris' law:

        $$
        \\frac{da}{dN} = \\text{intercept} \\, {\\Delta K)^{\\text{slope}}
        $$

        Parameters
        ----------
        stress_ : float
            Stress value.
        crack_depth_ : float
            Crack size value.

        Returns
        -------
        Tuple[float, float, float]
            Crack growth rate, stress intensity factor, and geometry factor.
        """

        sif, g_factor = get_sif(
            stress_, crack_depth_, self.crack_type, self.crack_geometry
        )
        knees = self.get_knees_sif
        if sif < self.threshold:
            the_growth_rate = 0.0
        elif sif >= self.critical:
            the_growth_rate = np.inf
        else:
            idx = np.digitize(np.asarray([sif]), knees, right=True) - 1
            the_growth_rate = self.intercept[idx][0] * sif ** self.slope[idx][0]

        return the_growth_rate, sif, g_factor

    def calc_crack_depth(
        self,
    ) -> Tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray, bool]:
        """Calculate the crack size and the SIF values.

        Using the assigned stress ranges and count-cycles arrays, the final
        crack size is calculated. The method iterates over the stress ranges
        and count-cycles arrays and updates the crack size at each step.

        ```python
        crack_depth = [crack_geometry["initial_depth"]]

        sifs = []
        factors = []
        for stress, count_cycle in zip(stress_range, count_cycle):
            for i in range(count_cycle):
                sifs.append(get_sif(stress,
                                    crack_depth, self.crack_geometry))
                factors.append(get_geometry_factor(crack_depth,
                                                   crack_type,
                                                   crack_geometry))
                if sifs[-1] >= critical:
                    return crack_depth, sifs, factors, True
                if sifs[-1] < threshold:
                    continue
                crack_depth.append(crack_depth[-1] +
                                   calc_growth_rate(stress, crack_depth))
        return crack_depth, sifs, factors, False
        ```

        Returns
        -------
        Tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray, bool]
            Crack size array, SIF array, geometric factor array, failure flag.
        """

        if self.crack_type not in ("HOL_CYL_01", "INF_SUR_00", "TABULATED"):
            raise NotImplementedError(
                "Geometric factor not implemented for assigned crack type."
            )
        dimensions = (
            cylinder_dimensions(self.crack_geometry)
            if self.crack_type == "HOL_CYL_01"
            else None
        )
        table = (
            (
                np.ascontiguousarray(
                    self.crack_geometry["depths"], dtype=float
                ),
                np.ascontiguousarray(
                    self.crack_geometry["factors"], dtype=float
                ),
            )
            if self.crack_type == "TABULATED"
            else None
        )
        crack_depth, sif, factor, self.termination, message = legacy_growth(
            (
                np.ascontiguousarray(self.stress_range, dtype=float),
                np.ascontiguousarray(self.count_cycle, dtype=float),
            ),
            (
                np.ascontiguousarray(self.slope, dtype=float),
                np.ascontiguousarray(self.intercept, dtype=float),
                np.ascontiguousarray(self.get_knees_sif[1:-1], dtype=float),
                (self.threshold, self.critical),
            ),
            self.crack_geometry["initial_depth"],
            dimensions,
            table,
        )
        print(message)
        failure = (
            self.termination != "geometry_limit"
            and len(crack_depth) < self.size
        )
        return crack_depth, sif, factor, failure

class CrackGrowth:
    """Accessor for the crack growth calculation

    Parameters
    ----------
    pandas_obj : pd.DataFrame
        The pandas dataframe object that will be extended
        with the crack growth calculation results. The original dataframe must
        have the following columns:
        - stress_range
        - count_cycle
        - mean_stress
        The dataframe will be extended with the following columns:
        - crack_depth
        - sif
        - cumul_cycles
        - geometry_factor
    """

    def __init__(self, pandas_obj):
        # self._validate(pandas_obj)
        self._obj = pandas_obj
        self.cg_curve = pandas_obj.attrs.get("cg_curve")
        self.crack_geometry = pandas_obj.attrs.get("crack_geometry")
        self.final_cycles = pandas_obj.attrs.get("final_cycles")

    @staticmethod
    def _validate(obj):
        """Validate the input DataFrame. Raise an error if the input
        DataFrame does not contain the right columns.
        """
        if {
            "crack_depth",
        }.issubset(obj.columns):
            e_msg = "'crack_depth' already calculated"
            raise AttributeError(e_msg)

        if not {"count_cycle", "mean_stress", "stress_range"}.issubset(
            obj.columns
        ):
            e_msg = "Must have 'count_cycle', 'mean_stress' and 'stress_range'."
            raise AttributeError(e_msg)

    def calc_growth(
        self,
        cg_curve: ParisCurve,
        crack_geometry: AbstractCrackGeometry,
        express_mode: bool = False,
    ):
        """Calculate the crack_propagation of the given crack geometry.

        Parameters
        ----------
        cg_curve : py_fatigue.ParisCurve
            The crack growth curve
        crack_geometry : AbstractCrackGeometry
            The initial crack geometry
        express_mode : bool, optional
            If True, the crack propagation runs 'express', by default False

        Returns
        -------
        DataFrame
            The DataFrame with the results of the crack_growth analysis.
        """

        self._validate(self._obj)

        # Handle the stress ranges and cycle counts as they might be clustered
        # in a matrix and we need to iterate over single or "little" cycles
        # within the same stress range block

        if np.max(self._obj["count_cycle"]) > 1:
            if express_mode:
                splts = [
                    10 ** int(np.log10(cycle))
                    for cycle in self._obj["count_cycle"].values
                ]
            else:
                splts = [
                    int(cycle) for cycle in self._obj["count_cycle"].values
                ]
            splt_cyc = [
                split(int(cycle), n) if int(cycle) > 0 else [cycle]
                for cycle, n in zip(self._obj["count_cycle"].values, splts)
            ]
            splt_len = [len(_) for _ in splt_cyc]

            count_cycle = np.asarray(
                [float(item) for sublist in splt_cyc for item in sublist]
            )
            stress_range = np.repeat(self._obj["stress_range"].values, splt_len)
        else:
            stress_range = self._obj["stress_range"].values
            count_cycle = self._obj["count_cycle"].values

        # Handle the geometry
        crack_type = str(crack_geometry._id)
        if crack_type not in ("HOL_CYL_01", "INF_SUR_00", "TABULATED"):
            raise ValueError("Unsupported crack geometry")
        geometry = numeric_geometry(crack_geometry)  # type: ignore

        cg_ = CalcCrackGrowth(
            stress_range,
            count_cycle,
            cg_curve.slope,
            cg_curve.intercept,
            float(cg_curve.threshold),
            float(cg_curve.critical),
            crack_type,
            geometry,
        )

        self._obj.attrs["cg_curve"] = cg_curve
        self._obj.attrs["crack_geometry"] = crack_geometry
        self._obj.attrs["final_cycles"] = cg_.final_cycles
        self.cg_curve = cg_curve
        self.crack_geometry = crack_geometry
        self._obj.final_cycles = cg_.final_cycles
        self.final_cycles = cg_.final_cycles

        cumul_cycle = np.cumsum(cg_.count_cycle[: len(cg_.crack_depth)])
        # manage the fact that clustered cycles are split in cg analysys
        # while the df keeps them clustered
        d_l = len(self._obj) - len(cg_.crack_depth)
        if d_l >= 0:
            self._obj["crack_depth"] = np.concatenate(
                (cg_.crack_depth, np.full(d_l, np.nan))
            )
            self._obj["sif"] = np.concatenate((cg_.sif, np.full(d_l, np.nan)))
            self._obj["cumul_cycle"] = np.concatenate(
                (cumul_cycle, np.full(d_l, np.nan))
            )
            self._obj["geometry_factor"] = np.concatenate(
                (cg_.geometry_factor, np.full(d_l, np.nan))
            )
        else:
            # perform a piecewise cumsum, as the cycles are clustered
            # and the crack growth is performed on the split cycles
            indices = np.cumsum(self._obj["count_cycle"].values).tolist()
            # Get the cumulative indices from count_cycles
            start_indices = [0] + indices[:-1]
            # Convert indices to integers
            indices = [int(idx) for idx in indices]
            start_indices = [int(idx) for idx in start_indices]
            # fmt: off
            crack_depth = np.array([cg_.crack_depth[start:end].sum()
                                    for start, end in zip(start_indices,
                                                          indices)])
            sif = np.array([cg_.sif[start:end].sum()
                            for start, end in zip(start_indices, indices)])
            cumul_cycle = np.array([cumul_cycle[start:end].sum()
                                    for start, end in zip(start_indices,
                                                          indices)])
            geometry_factor = np.array([cg_.geometry_factor[start:end].sum()
                                        for start, end in zip(start_indices,
                                                              indices)])
            # fmt: on
            self._obj["crack_depth"] = crack_depth
            self._obj["sif"] = sif
            self._obj["cumul_cycle"] = cumul_cycle
            self._obj["geometry_factor"] = geometry_factor
        self._obj.cg_curve = cg_curve
        self.cg_curve = cg_curve
        self._obj.crack_geometry = crack_geometry
        self.crack_geometry = crack_geometry
        return self._obj

        self,
        depth: Optional[PositiveFloat] = None,
        fig: Optional[matplotlib.figure.Figure] = None,
        ax: Optional[matplotlib.axes.Axes] = None,

        fig : matplotlib.figure.Figure, optional
            The figure to plot on.
        ax : matplotlib.axes.Axes, optional
            The axis to plot on.
        **kwargs : dict

        -------
        fig : matplotlib.figure.Figure
            The figure that was plotted on.
        ax : matplotlib.axes.Axes
        """

            depth = self.initial_depth
        angles = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 100)
        r_o = self.outer_diameter / 2
        r_i = r_o - self.thickness

        x_o = r_o * np.cos(angles)
        y_o = r_o * np.sin(angles)
        x_i = r_i * np.cos(angles)
        y_i = r_i * np.sin(angles)

        crack_width = self.width_to_depth_ratio * depth
        c_angle = crack_width / r_o  # crack angle
        c_v = r_o * np.sin(c_angle)
        a_h = depth - r_o * (1 - np.cos(c_angle))
        r_e = (c_v**2 + a_h**2) / (2 * a_h)  # radius of crack-front arc
        # angle of crack-front arc
        beta = np.arcsin(c_v / r_e)
        theta = np.linspace(-beta, beta, 100)  # θ (crack-front angle)
        phi = np.linspace(np.pi - c_angle, np.pi + c_angle, 100)

        # crack-front arc
        x_c = r_e * np.cos(theta) - (r_o + r_e - depth)
        y_c = r_e * np.sin(theta)
        x_c = np.append(x_c, r_o * np.cos(phi))
        y_c = np.append(y_c, r_o * np.sin(phi))

        axes.set_aspect("equal")

        axes.plot(x_i, y_i, c="k", lw=0.5, **kwargs)  # type: ignore
        axes.plot(x_c, y_c, c="r", lw=1.0, **kwargs)  # type: ignore
        return fig, axes

            one-element array.

            Figure and axes containing the sampled curve.

        minimum = np.asarray(k_min, dtype=float)

        sweep = maxima.ndim == 0

            if minimum.ndim != 0:

                )

            maxima = np.unique(

            label = "NASGRO growth rate"

    """Define a crack growth curve inthe form of Paris' law that can
    have an arbitrary number of slopes/intercepts as well as a
    threshold and critical stress intensity factor. For example:

    >>> #
    >>> #         ^                log da/dN - log ΔK
    >>> #         │                                *
    >>> #         │                                *
    >>> #         │                                *
    >>> #         │                                *
    >>> #         │                             *  .
    >>> #         │                          *  │  .
    >>> #         │                       *     │ m2
    >>> #         │                    *        │  .
    >>> #         │                 *-----------┘  .
    >>> #         │              *                 .
    >>> #         │           .*│                  .
    >>> #         │        . *  │ m1               .
    >>> #         │     .  *    │                  .
    >>> #         │  .   *------┘                  .
    >>> # (da/dN)1+    *                           .
    >>> #         │  . *                           .
    >>> #         │.   *                           .
    >>> # (da/dN)2+----|---------------------------|--------------->
    >>> #             ΔK0                         ΔKc
    >>> #                          Number of cycles

    The slope-intercept couples `((m1, log_a1), (m2, log_a2))`, and the
    endurance value `(Ne)` are the parameters necessary to fully describe this
    trilinear Paris' law. If the endurance is not set, it defaults to Inf.

    !!! note

        The curve can account for mean stress effect according to Walker's
        model. The walker_exponent is set to 0 by default, meaning no
        correction is applied, i.e. the base curve is valid for R=0.

    def get_knee_growth_rate(
        self,
        check_knee: Optional[Collection] = None,
        significant_digits: int = 2,
    ) -> np.ndarray:
        """Calculates the crack growth rate at the knee, if the Paris'
        law is more than linear.

        Parameters
        ----------
        check_knee : Iterable, optional
            Iterable of SIF values to check for the knee, by default None
        significant_digits : int, optional
            Number of significant digits to round the knee to, by default 2

        Returns
        -------
        np.ndarray
            knee crack growth rate
        """
        knee_growth_rate = np.empty(
            self.walker_intercept.size - 1, dtype=np.float64
        )
        if not self.linear:
            for i in range(self.walker_intercept.size - 1):
                m_i = self.slope[i + 1] / (self.slope[i + 1] - self.slope[i])
                m_i_p_1 = self.slope[i] / (self.slope[i + 1] - self.slope[i])
                knee_growth_rate[i] = (
                    self.walker_intercept[i] ** m_i
                    / self.walker_intercept[i + 1] ** m_i_p_1
                )
            if check_knee is not None:
                try:
                    iter(check_knee)
                except TypeError:
                    check_knee = np.asarray([check_knee])
                else:
                    check_knee = np.asarray(check_knee)
                if len(check_knee) != len(self.slope) - 1:  # type: ignore
                    raise ValueError(
                        f"{len(self.slope) - 1} knee points expected"
                    )
                check_knee_growth_rate = self.get_growth_rate(check_knee)
                _ = [
                    np.testing.assert_approx_equal(  # type: ignore
                        c_k_s, k_s, significant=significant_digits
                    )
                    for c_k_s, k_s in zip(
                        check_knee_growth_rate, knee_growth_rate
                    )
                ]
        else:
            if check_knee is not None:
                raise ValueError("0 knee points expected")
        return knee_growth_rate

Calculate the crack size by numerically integrating the crack growth curve. In this example we will consider the Paris' law, but the function can be used with any other crack growth curve.

\[ \begin{cases} \frac{da}{dN} & = C \left( \Delta K \right)^{m} \\ \Delta K & = Y(a) \cdot \Delta \sigma \sqrt{\pi a} \\ \end{cases} \]

From the equations above, the crack growths rate can be expressed as a function of the stress range. Considering the Paris' law, as in this example, gives:

\[ \frac{da}{dN} = C \left( Y(a) \cdot \Delta \sigma \sqrt{\pi a} \right)^{m} \]

se this crack growth rate together with the cycle step size to determine the incremental growth of the crack for this iteration:

\[ \Delta a = \frac{da}{dN} \cdot \Delta N \]

The new crack size is then calculated by adding the incremental growth to the previous crack size:

\[ a_{j+1} = a_{j} + \Delta a \]

where \(a_{j}\) is the crack size at the \(j\)-th cycle.

The crack is grown iteratively either until the failure condition is met or until the stress history is over.

If failure occurs, the total number of cycles that it took to grow the crack to the critical size becomes the predicted life of the part.

    # in a matrix and we need to iterate over single or "little" cycles
    # within the same stress range block
    intensity_unit = re.fullmatch(

        r"(?:√\s*(?:mm|cm|m|in)|sqrt\(\s*(?:mm|cm|m|in)\s*\))",

    """Propagate a crack through the load blocks in the supplied order.

    Use MPa, mm and MPa sqrt(mm) throughout. Controls set the maximum step

    total number of trial steps. History contains accepted states. At a

    intensity range for growth at the surface tip. History reports applied
    intensities.

    section, initial, names = _plate_state(geometry)

    raw, termination = ordered_growth(
        section,

        astuple(curve),

        (max_cycle_step, length_tolerance, cycle_tolerance, max_trials),

            tuple(k_min[:dimensions]),
            tuple(k_max[:dimensions]),
            block,

    )
    return GrowthResult(history, termination, names)
